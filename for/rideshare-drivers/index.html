How a rideshare shift adds up

A shift is dozens of short drives: to the pickup, the ride itself, then repositioning toward the next busy area, or the empty drive back from a far-off drop-off. Many drivers run more than one app at a time, so no single company sees the whole shift.

The summary a rideshare company sends at tax time is its record of your work on its platform, counted its way. It won’t include miles you drove for other apps, and it may not match every mile you drove for work. Your own log fills that gap.

What the IRS publishes

The IRS asks for records that show, for each business drive, the date, where you went, the business purpose and the miles, kept at or near the time of the drive. IRS Publication 463 explains what counts as an adequate record. Schedule C also asks whether you have written evidence for the vehicle miles you claim.

Which miles count, such as the drive from home to your first pickup, is a question for your tax advisor.

How HeyMile keeps up

  • Every drive, logged on its own. HeyMile detects your drives and logs the miles, even with the app closed. Set it to start a drive when your car connects, so drives are caught from the start.
  • Answer on CarPlay. Two big buttons on the car’s screen: Business and Personal. Tap one when you’re stopped at the curb between rides. There’s nothing to type.
  • Or on the Lock Screen. When a drive ends, the Lock Screen asks “Business or personal?” One tap, and you’re back in the rideshare app.
  • Add a missed drive by voice. Say “From the airport to downtown” and HeyMile measures the route with Apple Maps, then asks before it logs anything.
  • Reports when you need them. Export the month or the year as a PDF or a spreadsheet, with each trip’s date, route, purpose and miles, and your odometer readings.

Before your first drive: in the iPhone’s Settings, tap Apps, then HeyMile, then Location, and choose Always, with Precise Location on. Then check that the top of HeyMile’s first screen says Tracking on. Without both, HeyMile can’t log drives on its own.

Questions

Does HeyMile connect to my rideshare app?

No. HeyMile doesn’t sign in to any other app or read your earnings. It logs the drives your iPhone detects, and you decide which ones were business.

I drive for more than one app. Do I need more than one log?

No. HeyMile logs the drive, not the app, so the miles from every platform you drive for end up in one log and one report.

Do I have to answer after every ride?

HeyMile asks once for each drive it logs, on the Lock Screen, in the widget or on CarPlay. If you’re busy, answer later: open HeyMile, or tell it something like “Mark the trip to Main St as business”.
