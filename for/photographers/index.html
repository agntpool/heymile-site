Work that moves around

Photographers, videographers, DJs, florists and planners drive to wherever the work is: a client consultation, a venue walkthrough, a scouting trip for the right light, the rental house for a lens, then the event itself, often on a weekend and often far from home.

Because the schedule is irregular and work days look like weekends, it’s easy to forget which drives were for work by the time you sit down with your books.

Write it down when it happens

The IRS describes good records as ones made at or near the time of the drive, with the date, destination, business purpose and miles (IRS Publication 463). For a creative business, the purpose is often what makes a drive clearly business: “Venue walkthrough for the June wedding.”

How HeyMile keeps up with you

  • Nothing to remember. HeyMile detects drives and logs the miles, so the scouting drive on a Sunday is in the log.
  • Business, then the purpose. One tap on the Lock Screen, then a few words about the job.
  • Name the place. Say “From the studio to the botanical garden”: HeyMile finds it in Apple Maps, asks if it’s the right one, and measures the miles.
  • Find every trip to a venue. Search Spotlight by place or purpose.
  • See the year. Every trip on one map, business in blue and personal in green, and a report as a PDF or a spreadsheet.

Before your first drive: in the iPhone’s Settings, open HeyMile, then Location, and choose Always, with Precise Location on. Then check that the top of HeyMile’s first screen says Tracking on. Without both, HeyMile can’t log drives on its own.

Questions

Can I add a drive HeyMile missed?

Yes. Tell HeyMile where you drove; it measures the miles with Apple Maps and asks before it logs anything. You can also edit any trip from Trips.

Do I need to start HeyMile before a shoot?

No. HeyMile detects drives on its own. If you want to be sure, start a drive from the Drive control in Control Center or with the Action button.

What does my tax advisor get?

A PDF or a spreadsheet for the month or the year, with each trip’s date, route, purpose and miles, and your odometer readings.
