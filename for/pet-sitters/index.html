Short drives, all day

Pet care runs on routes: morning visits, midday walks, evening feedings, and overnight stays, often seven days a week. Each visit is a short drive, and short drives are the ones nobody remembers to write down.

Many pet sitters work on their own or through booking apps, and some take clients both ways. Either way, the miles are yours to keep track of.

What the IRS publishes

The IRS expects a record of each business drive, with its date, destination, purpose and miles, kept at or near the time (IRS Publication 463). A handful of two-mile drives a day adds up over a year.

How HeyMile keeps up

  • Every visit, logged. HeyMile detects drives and logs the miles, even with the app closed.
  • One tap at the curb. When you park, the Lock Screen asks “Business or personal?” Answer before you get the leash.
  • A widget that asks for you. On the Home Screen, the widget shows Business and Personal when a drive is waiting.
  • Ask your log. “How many miles this month?” HeyMile answers from your log.
  • One report. A PDF or a spreadsheet for the month or the year, with the drives for every client and booking app in one log.

Before your first drive: in the iPhone’s Settings, tap Apps, then HeyMile, then Location, and choose Always, with Precise Location on. Then check that the top of HeyMile’s first screen says Tracking on. Without both, HeyMile can’t log drives on its own.

Questions

I use a booking app for some clients. Does HeyMile connect to it?

No. HeyMile doesn’t connect to other apps. It logs the drives your iPhone detects, whoever booked the visit.

Will HeyMile catch very short drives?

A drive is kept when it is longer than 0.2 miles and one minute. You can add any drive it missed by telling HeyMile where you drove.

Can I mark a stop for my own errands as personal?

Yes. Every drive is Business or Personal, and reports keep them apart.
