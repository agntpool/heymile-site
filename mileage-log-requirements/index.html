What a mileage log should include, according to the IRS

Checked against irs.gov on November 21, 2026. Based on the edition of Publication 463 for 2025 returns.

IRS Publication 463 lists what your records should show for each business use of a car, and when to write it down. Here is a plain-language summary, with links to the source.

What each business drive should show

For car expenses, Publication 463 asks for:

  • The date of each business use of the car.
  • Your business destination.
  • The business purpose of the drive.
  • The mileage for each business use.
  • The total miles you drove the car during the year.

If you deduct actual expenses instead of using the standard rate, your records also need the cost of the car and any improvements, the date you started using it for business, and the cost of each expense.

Write it down at or near the time

Publication 463 says to record each business use at or near the time it happens, because a record kept at the time has more value than one prepared later from memory. You don’t have to write everything down the same day: a log kept weekly that accounts for the week’s driving counts as a timely kept record.

Paper, spreadsheet or app

The IRS accepts an account book, diary, log, statement of expense, trip sheets or a similar record, and Publication 463 says a record prepared on a computer is considered an adequate record. What matters is that it has the details above and was kept at the time.

How long to keep it

Generally, keep the records that support a deduction for 3 years from the date you file the return that claims it. Publication 463 and Publication 583 explain the exceptions.

Commuting isn’t business driving

Publication 463 says you can’t deduct commuting between your home and your main or regular place of work, no matter how far it is. Drives between work locations, to a temporary work location, or from a home office that is your principal place of business can be different. These rules depend on your situation, so a complete log of every drive, sorted business and personal, lets your tax advisor apply them to the facts.

Schedule C asks about written evidence

If you file Schedule C, Part IV asks whether you have evidence to support your car deduction and whether that evidence is written. The Schedule C instructions point to Publication 463 for the record-keeping rules.

Questions

What does the IRS require in a mileage log?

For each business use of a car: the date, the business destination, the business purpose and the miles, plus the total miles for the year. See IRS Publication 463.

Does a mileage log have to be on paper?

No. Publication 463 says a record prepared on a computer is considered an adequate record.

How often should I update my mileage log?

At or near the time of each drive. A log kept weekly that accounts for the week’s use is considered a timely kept record.

How long should I keep my mileage log?

Generally 3 years from the date you file the return that claims the deduction. Publication 463 and Publication 583 explain the exceptions.
